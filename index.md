## Signs your computer has a virus

You may have malware if you notice any of these:

- Pop-ups or ads appear even when your web browser is closed.
- Your browser's home page or search engine changed by itself.
- New toolbars, extensions or programs appeared that you didn't install.
- The computer suddenly became slow, hot or noisy for no reason.
- Your antivirus was turned off and won't turn back on.
- Friends received strange emails or messages from your accounts.
- A full-screen warning says your computer is "locked" and tells you to call a number.

**Important:** that last one is usually not a virus at all. It's a scam web page designed to look like a Microsoft or Apple warning. Don't call the number on it. Close the browser (on Windows, press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Esc</kbd> and end the browser task), and the "lock" disappears. Real security warnings never ask you to phone anyone.

## Free steps to remove a virus yourself

Do these in order. Stop when the problem is gone.

### Step 1: Disconnect and back up your important files

If you think something is actively stealing data, for example your accounts are sending messages you didn't write, disconnect the computer from the internet first by turning off Wi-Fi or unplugging the network cable. Then copy your important documents and photos to an external drive. Don't copy programs or downloads you don't recognize.

### Step 2: Run a full scan (Windows 11 and 10)

Windows includes Microsoft Defender antivirus at no cost.

1. Open the **Start** menu, type **Windows Security** and open it.
2. Select **Virus & threat protection**, then **Scan options**.
3. Choose **Full scan** and select **Scan now**. It can take an hour or more.
4. When it finishes, remove or quarantine everything it finds, then restart.

Microsoft explains each scan type in its guide to [virus and threat protection in the Windows Security app](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/security/threat-malware-protection/virus-and-threat-protection-in-the-windows-security-app).

### Step 3: Run the offline scan for stubborn infections (Windows)

Some malware hides while Windows is running. The offline scan restarts your computer and scans before Windows loads.

1. In **Windows Security > Virus & threat protection > Scan options**, choose **Microsoft Defender Offline scan**.
2. Select **Scan now**. The computer restarts, scans for about 15 minutes, then starts normally.

For a second opinion, Microsoft also offers the free [Microsoft Safety Scanner](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-endpoint/safety-scanner-download), a one-time scanning tool that works alongside your antivirus.

### Step 4: Remove unwanted programs and browser extensions

- **Windows:** open **Settings > Apps > Installed apps**. Sort by install date and uninstall anything you don't recognize that appeared around the time the problems started. Search the name online first if you're unsure.
- **Browser:** open your browser's extensions page (type `chrome://extensions` or `edge://extensions` in the address bar) and remove any extension you didn't add yourself. If pop-ups continue, use the browser's **Reset settings** option.

### Step 5: Clean up a Mac

Macs have built-in malware protection, but adware and unwanted apps still get in.

1. Open **Finder > Applications** and drag apps you don't recognize to the Trash.
2. Check **System Settings > General > Login Items** and remove anything unfamiliar.
3. Remove unknown extensions from Safari (**Safari > Settings > Extensions**) and any other browser you use.
4. Keep macOS up to date under **System Settings > General > Software Update**.

Apple explains how macOS protects you in [Protect your Mac from malware](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/protect-your-mac-from-malware-mh40596/mac).

### Step 6: Secure your accounts

Once the computer is clean, change the passwords for your email, banking and shopping accounts. If you're not sure the computer is clean yet, do this from a different device. Turn on two-step verification wherever it's offered. Start with your email, because it can reset all your other passwords.

The FTC's guide to [protecting against, detecting and removing malware](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/malware-how-protect-against-detect-and-remove-it) has more on what to do after an infection.

- **You let someone connect to your computer** after a scam call or pop-up. Assume they left something behind.
- **Your bank or email accounts were accessed**, or money is involved.

### How much does it cost to get a virus removed?

Prices vary by provider and by how serious the infection is. Some charge a flat fee, others a membership. Always ask for the total price before anyone starts work. At KeyBridge, you hear the price before we begin.

### Where can I get free virus removal?

Your computer already has free tools. On Windows, run a full scan and then a Microsoft Defender Offline scan in Windows Security (steps 2 and 3 above). Microsoft's Safety Scanner is also free. On a Mac, keep macOS updated and remove unknown apps and extensions (step 5). Paid help makes sense when the free tools don't fix it.

### Can a virus be removed remotely?

Yes, in most cases. A technician connects to your computer with your permission and runs the cleanup while you watch. Remote removal can't help if the computer won't start at all or has no internet connection.

### Is it a virus or a scam pop-up?

If a page says your computer is locked or infected and tells you to call a number, it's almost always a scam web page, not a real virus. Close the browser and don't call the number on the screen. If you're worried, contact a company you found yourself, not one from a pop-up.

### Should I reinstall Windows to get rid of a virus?

Only as a last resort. Most infections can be removed without wiping the computer. If a technician recommends a reinstall, they should first back up your files.
